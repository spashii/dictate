Privacy

Short version: Dictate needs no sign-up to download. You give your email once per Mac, in your browser, so I can send you a one-time activation code. You can ask me to delete it at any time.

What I store when you activate

  • Your email address, which is used to send your one-time activation code.
  • When you activated and on how many Macs, and which app version asked.
  • Which wording of the form you saw.
  • Where you came from, if the link you followed said so (for example a campaign name).
  • Older signups also keep whether their download link was sent and opened.

I do not store your IP address. To stop spam, I keep a salted, one-way hash of it for about two hours, which cannot be turned back into your address.

Your code

It is sent from dictate@firstversion.in. I keep only a one-way hash of the code, for 15 minutes. Replies reach me.

One cookie

Following the download link sets one cookie on dictate.firstversion.in for 30 days that remembers the campaign that brought you, so activation can count it. It holds nothing that identifies you.

Who handles it

The controller of your data is Sameer, the developer of Dictate. The site, its database and the spam check (Cloudflare Turnstile) run on Cloudflare. The code email is sent through a mail service. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages.

Deleting your data

Reply to any email from me and ask to be removed. I delete your email and activation record. You can also ask for a copy of what I hold, or for a correction.

The app itself

Your audio stays on your Mac. Dictate uses Sparkle to check for updates: a check asks apps.firstversion.in for the list of updates and tells it which version you have. The server counts checks by app, version and country and never stores your IP address. Updates download from dictate.firstversion.in, and the speech models download once from huggingface.co.

After you activate, one item sits in your login Keychain: a token signed by the Dictate site. It holds your signup id (a random code that points to your email in my list), a random number made on this Mac, and the date. Dictate checks the signature on this Mac and does not send the token anywhere.
