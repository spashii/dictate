Why I made it

I wanted fast, simple and robust dictation and transcription for calls, running on my Mac without sending data to the cloud. Local transcription on Apple silicon is so good now that there's simply no need to. Handy and OpenSuperWhisper are my all-time favorites, and I've learned a lot from them.

I noticed a few things that could be better for me. Sometimes I'd talk and the mic was off, so Dictate shows a level meter and your words as you talk. Sometimes I'd hit Escape by accident and lose what I'd said, so only Dictate's own shortcut stops a recording. And I wanted something lightweight, hackable and customizable, that could also record my online calls and let my agents read them.

Along the way I found that Whisper, the model that turns your speech into text, can tell which words it wasn't sure of. Having it listen to just those parts again catches words it got wrong, at almost no extra time. That became Dictate.

Questions

How is it different from Handy and OpenSuperWhisper?

At the core they are close: all three run on your Mac. Handy also runs Parakeet and works on Windows and Linux. What Dictate adds is retries, online calls with both sides apart, and a local MCP server for your agents.

What will it cost?

Free during the beta, then $49 once. The first 50 to activate keep it free for good.

Why Whisper and not Parakeet?

Retries use the confidence Whisper gives each part it writes, and key terms go into Whisper's prompt. Parakeet is faster, and I have not measured it against this pipeline.

Is it open source?

No. It is source available under FSL-1.1-MIT: you can read, change and run the code, but not offer it in a commercial product that competes with it. Each version becomes MIT two years after release, and versions up to 0.11.0 already are. The code and licence are on GitHub.

Does anything leave my Mac?

Your audio and transcripts stay on your Mac, in one folder, kept forever or for 30 days. Dictate goes online for its speech models (1.6 GB, once) and for updates, which count checks by app, version and country and never store your IP address. If you connect an agent, it can read your transcripts, and where it sends them depends on that agent. Where everything is kept

Why do I activate it?

Dictate is a free beta, and I want to know who uses it. Your email gets the code, and I might write to hear how you use Dictate and what's missing. Tell me to stop and I will. The app does not sign in and does not report what you do.

What does it ask permission for?

Microphone, to hear you. Accessibility, to paste into the app you are in; without it your text waits on the clipboard. Call audio, to hear the other side of an online call; it records only the call app's sound, and you can skip it and still dictate.

What can my agent see?

Your dictations and call transcripts, through the tools you leave on. The server listens only on your Mac and needs the token in Settings, Agents. Tools that record or change anything start off, because your agent reads what the other side of a call says, and that could steer it.

I use OpenSuperWhisper. Will they clash?

Both start on Option+`. Change one in Settings, Shortcuts. If OpenSuperWhisper already has the speech model, Dictate reuses it instead of downloading 1.6 GB again.

Which call apps work, and should I tell people?

Zoom, Teams, Slack, Discord, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Webex, Skype and Element, and calls in a browser tab such as Meet. Dictate does not tell the people on the other side apart. Tell them you are recording, because the law differs by country.

Which languages?

Whisper detects the language. Large v3 (3.1 GB) is there for languages where turbo does worse.
